Treatment of Civilian Upper Extremity Trauma Patients at a Military Level
One Trauma Center: Avoiding the “Walker Dip”

DISCUSSION

  • The United States military has reached unprecedented survival rates during recent conflicts, largely due to the surgical expertise developed in managing complex combat injuries.
  • During peacetime, this expertise often declines due to a significant drop in case volume and complexity, a phenomenon known as The Walker Dip.
  • With the possibility of near-pear conflicts, maintaining the operational readiness of military surgeons is now more critical than ever.
  • Upper extremity orthopedic trauma plays a significant role in combat care, accounting for nearly 10% of all combat related procedures in recent conflicts.
  • We set out to evaluate Brooke Army Medical Center’s ability to maintain the surgical readiness of their upper extremity surgeons and avoid the Walker Dip, given its unique role as the Department of Defense’s only level 1 trauma center.
  • The Knowledge, Skills, and Abilities (KSA) metric was utilized to provide an objective readiness score that prioritizes high acuity cases and volume to determine a surgeon’s deployment preparedness.
  • We also explore potential systems—such as expanded military-civilian partnerships and trauma center integration, to safeguard against the Walker Dip and ensure surgeons remain deployment-ready.

METHODS

  • Analyzed surgical case logs of Brooke Army Medical Center’s upper extremity orthopedic surgeons from 2014-2025.
  • To account for staffing fluctuations (from deployments, retirements, and PCS turnover), individual monthly case volumes were extrapolated to standardized 12-month case volumes.
  • Applied the Knowledge, Skills, and Abilities (KSA) metric using a case mix of 10% tier one and 90% tier four cases. This case mix was chosen as a conservative estimate for annual KSA scores per upper extremity surgeon.
  • The scores were compared against the 35,000 point deployment readiness benchmark.

Revised Knowledge, Skills, and Abilities (KSA) scoring system:

Tier 1 (average KSA score of 436): open debridements, open treatment of fractures, amputations, fasciotomies, external fixation

Tier 2 (average KSA score of 373): soft tissue, hand, spine, and foot procedures; arthroplasties and major tumor resection

Tier 3 (KSA score of 235): arthroscopy

Tier 4 (average KSA score of 161): closed treatment of fractures, hardware removal, other procedures not included above

  • During the period from 2014 to 2025, BAMC upper extremity surgeons performed 7706 cases, with this data serving as evidence that BAMC likely far exceeds the KSA deployment readiness benchmark for their upper extremity surgeons every year during this period.
  • Given the highly conservative estimate of 10% tier 1 and 90% tier 4 cases, BAMC’s high acuity, high volume case load likely places their surgeons far above the 35,000 KSA benchmark.
  • These findings highlight the critical role that high-acuity trauma exposure plays in preserving surgical capability to avoid the Walker Dip.

Strategic Imperative: Expand Access to Trauma Exposure

  • Many, if not most other medical treatment facilities (MTFs) may not achieve this level of acuity and volume and therefore could be falling short of this readiness benchmark.
  • Preventing the Walker Dip is a strategic necessity, not just a clinical concern. As we prepare for the next conflict, we cannot afford to re-learn hard lessons at the cost of service members’ lives.
  • Transformation of more MTFs into level one trauma centers is likely not feasible.
  • A more immediate and readily scalable solution lies in embedding military orthopedic surgeons in civilian level one trauma centers either as permanent assignments or targeted pre-deployment rotations.
  • The U.S. military has initiated similar collaborations, but administrative hurdles remain. Overcoming these hurdles to expand upon military-civilian trauma integration must be a DoD priority to preserve the surgical skills of our surgeons.

Background / Introduction

  • The United States military has reached unprecedented survival rates during recent conflicts, largely due to surgical expertise developed in managing complex combat injuries.
  • During peacetime, this expertise often declines as case volume and complexity drop—a phenomenon known as the Walker Dip. With the possibility of near-peer conflicts, maintaining military surgeons’ operational readiness is critical.
  • Upper extremity orthopaedic trauma accounts for nearly 10% of all combat-related procedures in recent conflicts.
  • This study evaluates Brooke Army Medical Center’s ability to maintain readiness of its upper extremity surgeons as the Department of Defense’s only Level I trauma center.
  • The Knowledge, Skills, and Abilities (KSA) metric provides an objective readiness score that prioritizes case acuity and volume in assessing deployment preparedness.

The team also considers military–civilian partnerships and trauma center integration as ways to preserve deployment-ready surgical skills.

Methods

  • Analyzed surgical case logs for BAMC upper extremity orthopaedic surgeons from 2014 to 2025.
  • To account for deployments, retirements, and PCS turnover, individual monthly case volumes were extrapolated to standardized 12-month case volumes.
  • Applied the revised KSA metric using a conservative estimated case mix of 10% Tier 1 and 90% Tier 4 cases.
  • Compared scores with the 35,000-point deployment readiness benchmark.

Revised KSA scoring system

  • TIER 1 · AVG. 436
    Open debridements, open fracture treatment, amputations, fasciotomies, and external fixation.
  • TIER 2 · AVG. 373
    Soft tissue, hand, spine, and foot procedures; arthroplasties and major tumor resection.
  • TIER 3 · 235
    Arthroscopy.
  • TIER 4 · AVG. 161
    Closed fracture treatment, hardware removal, and other procedures not included above.

Results

Across 2014–2025, BAMC upper extremity surgeons performed 7,706 cases. Estimated annual KSA scores exceeded the deployment readiness benchmark in every study year.

Discussion

BAMC likely exceeded the KSA deployment readiness benchmark for upper extremity surgeons each year from 2014 through 2025.

  • The conservative 10% Tier 1 / 90% Tier 4 estimate still places annual scores above 35,000 KSA points.
  • These findings highlight the role of high-acuity trauma exposure in preserving surgical capability and avoiding the Walker Dip.

Strategic imperative: expand access to trauma exposure

  • Many military treatment facilities may not reach this level of acuity and volume and could fall short of the readiness benchmark.
  • Transforming more facilities into Level I trauma centers is likely not feasible. A more scalable approach is embedding military orthopaedic surgeons in civilian Level I trauma centers, either through permanent assignments or targeted pre-deployment rotations.
  • Administrative hurdles remain. Expanding military–civilian trauma integration should be a DoD priority to preserve surgical skills.

The authors frame readiness as both a clinical and strategic necessity as the military prepares for future conflict.
